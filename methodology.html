Methodology

Controlled implementation,
measured end to end.

The Nexus methodology is designed to keep technology enthusiasm from outrunning business evidence, data boundaries, human ownership, testing, or written scope.

01

Diagnose

Current workflow, baseline KPI, burden, systems, risk, and process owner.

02

Design

Target state, data flow, human approvals, acceptance criteria, and assumptions.

03

Implement

Build the smallest controlled intervention justified by the agreed scope.

04

Train

SOPs, ownership, exception handling, escalation, and operational handoff.

05

Measure

Compare agreed post-launch metrics to the documented baseline and note limitations.

06

Optimize

Tune, maintain, expand, or retire based on observed evidence and approved changes.

Human-Only

Material judgment stays human

High-impact or specialist decisions remain with authorized people unless a written, lawful control model expressly provides otherwise.

AI Assist

AI informs; a human decides

AI may retrieve, summarize, classify, analyze, or suggest without owning the decision.

Draft + Review

Release requires approval

AI prepares an output; a designated reviewer accepts, changes, or rejects it.

Controlled Automation

Narrow pre-authorized actions

Low-risk actions may execute inside documented limits, logs, exception paths, and disable controls.

The Nexus evidence standard

Every result needs a chain of evidence.

A credible result should show what was happening before, exactly what changed, how the change was controlled, what was measured afterward, and what the evidence does—or does not—support.

Step 1

Business problem

A specific repeatable operating constraint with a responsible owner and clear business context.

Step 2

Baseline

The current-state metric, source, period, assumptions, and known data limitations before implementation.

Step 3

Intervention

What changed, which systems were involved, the implementation scope, and material dependencies.

Step 4

Human controls

Approval boundaries, exception handling, monitoring, fallback, and override responsibility.

Step 5

Observed result

Post-launch KPI evidence measured over a stated window rather than implied from a one-time event.

Step 6

Attribution limits

A clear statement of what the available evidence supports and what cannot responsibly be attributed to Nexus.

What can be measured

Make improvement visible in business terms.

The exact KPIs depend on the workflow, but Nexus structures measurement around practical operating outcomes.

Time & throughput

  • Handling time
  • Cycle time
  • Hours recovered
  • Volume processed

Quality & reliability

  • Error rate
  • Rework
  • Exception volume
  • Completion rate

Commercial impact

  • Response time
  • Follow-up completion
  • Lead conversion
  • Revenue influenced

Operating visibility

  • Reporting time
  • Data completeness
  • Decision latency
  • Adoption rate
