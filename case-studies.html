The Relystra evidence standard

Every result needs a chain of evidence.

A credible case study should show what was happening before, exactly what changed, how the change was controlled, what was measured afterward, and what the evidence does—or does not—support.

01

Business problem

A specific repeatable operating constraint with a responsible owner and clear business context.

02

Baseline

The current-state metric, source, period, assumptions, and known data limitations before implementation.

03

Intervention

What changed, which systems were involved, the implementation scope, and material dependencies.

04

Human controls

Approval boundaries, exception handling, monitoring, fallback, and override responsibility.

05

Observed result

Post-launch KPI evidence measured over a stated window rather than implied from a one-time event.

06

Attribution limits

A clear statement of what the available evidence supports and what cannot responsibly be attributed to Relystra.

What a published case study will show

Evidence register

Rather than presenting a single headline number, Relystra will preserve the information required to understand how the result was produced.

Baseline recordMetric definition, source, date range, assumptions, owner, and known limitations.
Implementation recordWorkflow changes, integrations, automation boundaries, testing, and deployment dates.
Control recordHuman approvals, exception logic, monitoring, fallback, and security constraints.
Measurement recordPost-launch KPI values, measurement window, source, comparison method, and material confounders.
Client authorizationSpecific approval before publishing client identity, quotations, or non-public business information.
Next decisionWhat the observed evidence justified improving, expanding, changing, or stopping next.
