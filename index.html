01 · 36 seconds

Where the Estimate Actually Stops

Missing photographs and unanswered scope questions can turn the owner’s pricing time into follow-up time. The real hold is the readiness handoff into pricing.

Read transcript

Estimate stuck before it reaches the customer? Nepsis maps, improves, tests and hands over one recurring workflow. Follow this estimate from request to sent. Now watch where the estimate stops.

The owner’s tray is not just full of pricing work. One pack has measurements but no photographs. Another has a scope question with no named person to answer it. A third looks complete until pricing begins. The owner is receiving work that is nearly ready, then spending attention chasing what is missing. The hold is the readiness handoff into pricing.

02 · 36 seconds

Requested Is Not Collected

A green status cannot decide what happened in the real world. Useful workflows define the state, the evidence and who owns the next action.

Read transcript

Collection requested. Equipment still on site. Nepsis shows why a working demo is not a handoff—and what the team needs before takeover. The screen is not the workflow.

The team first needs clear states. Ready for collection means site use has finished. Collection requested means the supplier has been contacted. Collected means the machine has physically left. Supplier confirmation received is another state where the applicable process requires it. The relevant hire terms and confirmations still need to be checked; a button does not decide them.

03 · 59 seconds

The Handoff Test Most Demos Skip

A clean demo is not the finish line. The handoff works when the people doing the job can run the normal route, own the next action and handle the exception.

Read transcript

A screen can pass a demo while the operation still fails. Nepsis tests the people, states, decisions and exceptions together. The site supervisor runs the normal case without the consultant narrating every click: identify the machine, record that it is ready, make or record the collection request and watch the office receive the correct state.

The route works when the people doing the job can follow it without being coached through every move. Then the consultant steps away. The user owns the next action. Handoff includes the operating note, named owner, normal path, fallback and exception route. The consultant leaves the path; the team keeps running it.
